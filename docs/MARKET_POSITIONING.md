# Resin Market Positioning

## Position

**Resin is the follow-through system for overloaded minds.**

For people who already know what matters but still struggle to begin, Resin turns one messy intention into a realistic next action, places it in the day, and protects the attention needed to start.

## Category

Resin should lead with **follow-through system**, not task manager, AI planner, calendar, or app blocker.

Those categories describe individual features. Follow-through describes the outcome created by the complete Resin loop:

1. Capture the unsorted thought.
2. Shape it into a concrete next action.
3. Place it in realistic available time.
4. Protect that action from selected distractions.
5. Reflect and improve the next plan.

## Ideal Customer

The primary customer is an independent knowledge worker, creator, student, or founder with high cognitive load and recurring task-initiation friction.

They are not looking for a larger productivity system. They already have notes, tasks, and a calendar. Their problem is the gap between knowing and doing:

- Important work remains vague for too long.
- Planning becomes another form of avoidance.
- Calendar blocks do not prevent distraction.
- App blockers remove temptation without reconnecting them to meaningful work.
- Complex systems create more maintenance than momentum.

Resin can resonate with ADHD and neurodivergent customers, but marketing should not diagnose users or imply treatment. Lead with the universal experience of overload, avoidance, and difficulty starting.

## Competitive Wedge

| Category | What it does well | Where it stops | Resin advantage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Task managers | Store and organize intentions | The user must still decide what to do now | Resin recommends a concrete next move |
| AI calendars | Place defined work into available time | They assume the task is already clear and the user will comply | Resin starts from an unsorted thought and protects execution |
| Visual planners | Make schedules easier to understand | Visibility does not remove digital escape hatches | Resin binds the visible next step to active protection |
| App blockers | Remove access to distractions | Blocking is disconnected from meaningful intent | Resin shows what the user chose to do and returns them to it |
| AI task breakdown tools | Make large tasks feel smaller | The resulting checklist remains passive | Resin carries the first step into scheduled, protected focus |

The unique mechanism is **intent-linked protection**: Resin does not merely block distractions. It protects a specific action the user chose to move forward.

## Message Hierarchy

### Primary promise

**Turn what you mean to do into what you actually start.**

### Product explanation

Resin turns a messy thought into one realistic next step, finds time for it, and blocks the apps and sites most likely to pull you away.

### Emotional outcome

Less negotiating with yourself. More visible momentum.

### Supporting proof

- One thought becomes a clear, time-estimated plan.
- The next action stays visible during focus.
- Apple Screen Time and Chrome protection shield selected distractions.
- Focus state follows the user across iPhone, web, and Chrome.
- AI is optional and selected Screen Time activity stays on-device.

## Voice

Resin should sound calm, precise, and non-judgmental.

Use:

- start, next move, follow through, protect, lighter, realistic, one thing
- direct descriptions of what happens next
- permission to begin imperfectly
- specific, restrained trust language

Avoid:

- hustle, optimize everything, supercharge, crush your goals
- vague AI-first claims
- shame about screen time or unfinished work
- diagnostic claims about ADHD
- broad “all-in-one productivity” positioning
- promising that Resin completes work for the user

## Funnel

### Acquisition

Lead with the task-initiation problem, then demonstrate the complete loop. The first CTA should promise a small outcome: **Start my first plan**.

### Signup

Keep social authentication focused and explain that Calendar and Screen Time access come later, at the moment they create value.

### Activation

The first-run experience must produce value before requesting permissions:

1. User enters one postponed task.
2. Resin reveals a realistic next move.
3. Resin explains protection and trust boundaries.
4. User begins a ten-minute focus or saves the plan.

### Conversion

Free proves the loop on iPhone. Pro sells continuity and stronger follow-through across web and Chrome—not more planning features for their own sake.

## Evidence Base

- Sunsama positions around calm, guided daily planning for modern professionals: https://www.sunsama.com/
- Tiimo owns visual, neuroinclusive planning and AI task breakdown: https://www.tiimoapp.com/
- Opal owns premium screen-time blocking and focus controls: https://opalapp.com/pricing
- one sec owns behavior interruption and reduced app opening: https://one-sec.app/
- Goblin Tools offers lightweight task breakdown and brain-dump compilation: https://goblin.tools/

Resin should not outclaim these products inside their established categories. It should own the connective outcome they leave fragmented: moving from intention to protected action.

## Experiments

1. Test “Planning is easy. Starting is the hard part.” against “Turn what you mean to do into what you actually start.”
2. Test “Start my first plan” against “Make one thing lighter.”
3. Measure landing-to-signup conversion by source and device.
4. Measure signup-to-first-thought, first-thought-to-plan, and plan-to-focus activation separately.
5. Ask activated users which problem brought them in: overwhelm, task breakdown, scheduling, distraction, or cross-device continuity.
6. Add real proof only after sufficient data: median time to first plan, protected minutes completed, and percentage of plans started.
